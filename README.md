# Grid Keeper

A tabletop AR management puzzle for iPhone. Place the small bay town of Brightwater on a real table, then keep its lights on for as many days as you can by building solar panels, wind turbines and batteries around the weather and the clock.

Built in Unity 6 with AR Foundation and ARKit as the vertical slice of the Grid Keeper Game Design Document.

## Features

- **Scan and place:** a dot field shows the detected table, and a dashed amber footprint shows where the town will land. Tap to anchor it.
- **Build:** pick Solar ($150), Wind ($200) or Battery ($120). The first tap shows a ghost preview and the second tap builds with a haptic tick. Remove refunds half the price.
- **Endless run:** each in-game day lasts 40 seconds and the days never stop. Demand grows 4% a day (up to double). Solar follows the sun, wind follows the weather (+40% on hills, -20% next to another turbine), and batteries store 30 MWh at 8 MW.
- **Weather:** random weather every day (clear, cloudy, windy or heatwave), with today and tomorrow on the HUD. Heatwaves raise demand by 30%.
- **Score and lives:** 10 points per MWh delivered, plus a bonus for every A, B or C day (300, 150, 50). A day under 55% of demand costs one of three lives. The best score is saved on the phone.
- **Blackouts:** when supply falls short, buildings go dark in priority order. The hospital stays lit longest, then houses, shops and the factory. A red edge pulses around the screen.
- **Results:** score and best score, days survived, a grade from A to D for the whole run, the council's note and a chart of the last days.
- **HUD:** a status pill (town powered, surplus or short), a meter for how much demand you are meeting, the score, and day plus lives.
- **Tutorial:** five story cards explain the goal in plain words, then Keeper's Notes guide the first days and pause the clock at dusk until you build a battery.
- **Day and night:** the sun moves with the clock, windows glow at night, clouds cast shadows and the factory chimney smokes during working hours.
- **Audio:** day and night ambience, a wind loop that follows wind speed, and cues for dawn, dusk, building, blackouts, power restored and results.

## Getting started

1. Clone the repository.
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/dduru26/grid-keeper.git
   ```
2. In Unity Hub choose **Add › Add project from disk** and select the `grid-keeper` folder. Open it with Unity 6000.4.7f1.
3. Open `Assets/GridKeeper/Scenes/Main.unity`.

The scene, prefabs and data assets are generated by Editor scripts. To rebuild them from scratch use **Grid Keeper › Setup › Build Main Scene**.

## Testing in the Editor (XR Simulation)

XR Simulation is enabled for the Editor, so the AR flow works without a phone.

1. Press **Play**. A simulated room loads and plane detection starts.
2. In the Game or Simulator view, hold the **right mouse button** and use **W A S D** to move and **Q / E** to go down and up. Look at the floor until the dot field appears.
3. When the prompt reads **Tap to place Brightwater**, left-click to place the town.
4. Use the mouse scroll wheel to scale the town, as a pinch does on the phone.

For a phone-shaped view, switch the Game view to **Simulator** and choose **iOS Notch Device**.

## Building to iPhone

1. **Grid Keeper › Build › iOS Xcode Project** (or **iOS Development Build** for Profiler and the FPS meter). The Xcode project is written to `Builds/iOS`.
2. Open `Builds/iOS/Unity-iPhone.xcodeproj` in Xcode.
3. Select the **Unity-iPhone** target, open **Signing & Capabilities**, tick **Automatically manage signing** and pick your team.
4. Choose your iPhone as the destination and press **Run**.
5. On first install, trust the developer profile on the phone in **Settings › General › VPN & Device Management**.

## How to play

1. Move the phone slowly over a table until the dots appear, then tap to place Brightwater.
2. Read the story cards and the council's forecast for today and tomorrow, then press **Plan the grid**.
3. Build a few sources and press **Start the shift**. The run starts on a 12 by 12 town with $1,300.
4. Watch the HUD: day and lives, the status pill and meter, the score, today's and tomorrow's weather, and the chart.
5. Spend what you earn ($3 per MWh delivered) to prepare for evenings and bad weather.
6. Each day is graded: A at 90% of demand met, B at 75%, C at 55%, D below that. A D day costs a life. Lose three and the run ends, or end it yourself from the pause menu, and try to beat your best score.

## Architecture

| Pattern or principle | Where |
|---|---|
| State | `GameState` with `PlacingState`, `BriefingState`, `PlayingState`, `PausedState` and `ResultsState`, driven by `GameManager` |
| Inheritance and polymorphism | `PowerSource` is abstract. `SolarPanel` and `WindTurbine` each supply their own output rule |
| Interfaces (dependency inversion) | `EnergyGrid` only knows `IGenerator` and `IStorage`, so a new source type needs no change to the grid, and the tests can use fakes |
| Factory | `SourceFactory` creates and removes sources, so the toolbar never instantiates prefabs itself |
| Observer | C# events (`Ticked`, `PhaseChanged`, `BlackoutChanged`, `DayCompleted`, `ShiftEnded`, `Built`, `CityPlaced`) drive the UI, audio and tutorial without them polling the game |
| Data-driven design (open/closed) | Every tuning value lives in ScriptableObjects (`BalanceConfig`, `PlaceableDefinition`, `BuildingDefinition`, `WeatherProfile`, `CityLayout`), so balance changes need no code changes |
| Single responsibility | `RunSimulation` runs the simulation as a plain C# class, `RunRules` holds the day rules, `EnergyGrid` only balances a tick, `GameClock` only keeps time, `GameManager` only runs the game flow, the views only render |
| Explicit dependencies | References are wired in the Inspector by the scene builder. `Require.Reference` logs a clear error if one is missing, instead of searching the scene |
| Encapsulation | `BatteryStore` owns its stored energy and the 8 MW per-tick rate, and `Wallet` owns the money |

## Tests

Open **Window › General › Test Runner**, choose **EditMode** and press **Run All**. 24 tests cover the energy grid, batteries, solar output, the clock, grades, day bonuses and lives, demand growth, stats, the wallet and the endless forecast.

## Performance

Profiled on iPhone XR against a **60 FPS** target. The full write-up is in the Performance Considerations section of the GDD, and the Profiler captures are in `Docs/performance`.

- IL2CPP (Master configuration) for ARM64, managed and engine code stripping, incremental GC
- URP mobile asset: SRP Batcher on, HDR off, MSAA off, render scale 0.85, one shadow cascade with a 512 shadow map, 1.5 m shadow distance for a tabletop scene, no additional lights, no depth or opaque textures
- HUD text only rebuilds when a displayed value changes, which removes the per-tick garbage spikes
- Static batching: the board and buildings are combined with `StaticBatchingUtility.Combine` once the town is anchored, so the anchor can still move
- GPU instancing enabled on all shared materials, and all models share a small palette of materials
- Mipmaps and ASTC compression on textures, capped at 256 px
- Audio: loops as Vorbis compressed in memory, one-shots as ADPCM decompressed on load, all mono
- `Application.targetFrameRate = 60` with vSync off, accelerometer disabled, multithreaded rendering on
- LOD and occlusion culling are not used: every model is under 200 triangles and the whole town is always in view, so they would add cost without saving any

## Scope

This build contains the Brightwater town only. An online leaderboard for the best score is the next step. The Harbor and Ridge towns, wind streak effects, multiplayer, cloud saves and Android are planned for a later version.
